
\chapter{Conclusion}
This thesis has explored how exposure to international maritime trade shapes the likelihood of conflict across Nigerian regions. The analysis combined information on port level activity, maritime connectivity, and geography to build an index of local exposure to global trade shocks. The evidence points to a heterogeneous pattern: the effect of trade on conflict varies widely across space, sectors, and local resource conditions.

\section{Main findings}
\textit{Baseline and falsification.} For commercial ports, the aggregate effect of the exposure index is small on average, but analysing the heterogeneity of the sample given the development of each ADM2 (proxied by nightlights), we discovered that in relatively less developed areas, higher exposure to trade is associated with lower conflict, while effects in more developed areas are near zero. When index is studied including only ports without commercial relevance, coefficients become weaker and not statistically significant. This falsification supports the interpretation that the mechanism runs through international trade, in particolar access to international market, rather than mere proximity to coastal infrastructure. \\

\textit{Geographic heterogeneity.} We studied different heterogeneity to study how the relationship varies across different dimension. Results show that there is a stabilizing effect stronger in the eastern belt than in the west. This spatial asymmetry matches economic patterns: eastern ADM2s are more rural, as shown by the proxy of nightlights, making them more sensitive to trade-induced welfare improvements.\\

\textit{Sector and trade flow heterogeneity.} Result shows that the negative association is more pronounced for ports oriented toward the one that trade vegetables, as second most traded industry, and imports more with respect of what they export. This is consistent with our theory of consumption-smoothing channel.\\

\textit{Tribes.} Taking into account for tribal domanince and fractorization, proxied by ethnicity. We saw that the coefficients are positive but not statistically significant. \\

\textit{Resource endowments (water).} Interactions with water availability (a proxy for agricultural potential) indicate that the pacifying effect concentrates in resource-constrained areas. Where water availability is higher—hence greater agricultural rents or competition—the relationship can weaken or even turn positive, suggesting that trade may intensify distributional tensions when valuable rents are at stake. \\


\section{Interpretation}
Overall, the findings support that consuption related mechanism are more directly influences by trade exogenous shock, especially in poorer ADM2. \\
In contrast, where economic rents and pre existing advantages are larger, trade and access to ports may lose significane and in some case can show signals that could have a destabilizing effect. In short, trade is stabilising where markets fill and income opportunities, while it risks destabilising where it amplifies returns and contestable surpluses. 

\section{Limitations}
While the findings are robust across specifications reported here, several caution should be taking in consideration. \\

First measurement. CPT and LSCI are new port indicators available for a shorter window, for which we built to the study period and the other port indicators, sectoral and flow classifications rely on IMF PortWatch assumption. Night-lights and water availability are proxies for income and agricultural potential rather than direct measures.\\
Second, we used inverse distance to the nearest port aiming to abstract from the actual situation of infrastructures. In this way we didn't take into account for road connections and trasports nework, future work should embed explicit transport networks and travel times. \\
Third conflict counts aggregate heterogeneous events; although the interpretation emphasizes consumption-related mechanisms, misclassification remains possible.\\
Lastely, the design mitigates simultaneity by using lagged port shocks and fixed effects, and the “all-ports” falsification helps; still, unobserved time varying shocks correlated with both exposure and conflict and the risk of reverse causality  cannot be fully ruled out.

\section{Policy implications}
The heterogeneity of effects observed across regions suggests that how a country integrates its different regions and sector into global markets is as important as the extent of its trade openness. Policies should focus on the qualitative dimensions of integration such us inclusivenes and institutional capacity. \\

In particular, the findings indicate that trade infrastructure and redistributive policies are crucial to prevent the concentration of gains in port-proximate areas, because where trade activity is most intense, improving local governance and transparency is an important channel that can be used to mitigate distributional tensions, increasing the opportunity cost of violence. \\
Conversely, in inland regions an effective policy should be reducing effective distance to ports investing in infrastructure. In this way, the stabilizing effect of trade exposure could be amplifued by making accessible the global market and adding more economic diversification. \\
Finally, a crucial policy is the intervention in more developed or areas with more water availability, in which trade expansion should be accompanied by institutional mechanisms that prevent rapacity based conflict.\\

\section{External validity and future research}
Although Nigeria’s coastal geography and regional fragmentation make it a compelling test case, external validity should be assessed cautiously. \\
Extending the approach to other coastal African economies and to land linked settings would clarify the role of transport networks. \\
In particular, four concrete extensions emerge: (i) extention to all Nigeria ADM2, and not only to the coastal ones. (ii) Network based exposure, allowing exposure to multiple ports and it could be added the possibility to weight the port based on their economic relevance. 
(iii) Micro-outcomes such as link exposure to prices and employment to pin down the welfare more directly. 
(iv) Institutional moderators: interact exposure with local governance quality, education and curruption.

\section{Contribution}
Empirically, the thesis aim to contribute to the literatiure introducing a geography exposure indec that link port level shocks and connectivity to local conflict dynamics, accounting for heterogeneity by development, location, sector, tribes and resource endowment. \\
It aim to ptove a subnational evaluation of trade to shock moving beyond national averages. Results suggest that trade could be a pacifing channel, but not everywhere and not unconditionally. Thus, trade openness contributes to stability only when broadens economic opportunities as its gains are inclusive and not captured by narrow interests, while integration that amplifies rent competition can sustain or even intensify conflict. Effective governance must therefore address this conditional channel. Recognizing this structural asymmetry is essential for policy design.
